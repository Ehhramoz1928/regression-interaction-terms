The primary objective of this study was to evaluate the complex relationships affecting vehicle fuel efficiency using the mtcars dataset, which contains technical specifications for 32 different automobiles. This report specifically investigates how fuel economy, measured in miles per gallon, is influenced by the interaction between a quantitative variable, vehicle weight, and a qualitative categorical variable, transmission type. By employing multiple regression with interaction terms, this study determines if the impact of weight on efficiency is mediated or changed by whether a vehicle is automatic or manual.  

The highly significant interaction term explicitly proves that the marginal impact of weight on fuel economy depends dynamically on whether the vehicle uses an automatic or manual transmission. Specifically, for every 1,000 lb increase in weight, automatic cars see a decrease of 3.79 mpg, while manual cars see a much steeper decrease of approximately 9.08 mpg. With a recorded Adjusted R-squared of 0.8151, the model explains 81.51 percent of the total variance in vehicle fuel efficiency. Diagnostic plots generated to test the underlying statistical assumptions support the assumption of homoscedasticity and provide solid evidence that the error terms are normally distributed. 

The implementation uses the R programming language inside the Jupyter Notebook framework. The statistical methodology includes Multiple Linear Regression, Ordinary Least Squares Evaluation, Hypothesis Testing including F-tests and t-tests, and Residual Diagnostics. The mtcars collection contains 32 observations across 11 distinct variables. The dependent variable is mpg, representing fuel economy in miles per gallon. The quantitative independent predictor is wt, representing vehicle weight recorded in units of 1,000 lbs. The qualitative categorical predictor is am, representing transmission type where 0 indicates automatic and 1 indicates manual.

The model expresses the relationship between fuel economy, weight, and transmission as a single calculation where miles per gallon equals 31.4161 minus 3.7858 multiplied by weight, plus 14.8784 multiplied by transmission, minus 5.2984 multiplied by the interaction of weight and transmission.  Regarding global model utility, the framework is highly significant with an overall global F-test p-value of 5.209 times 10 to the power of negative 11, confirming that the model provides a statistically significant fit to the data at the 0.05 alpha level. Individual t-tests verify that vehicle weight with a p-value of 0.0001, transmission type with a p-value of 0.0014, and their interaction term with a p-value of 0.0011 are all mathematically meaningful predictors, fully justifying their inclusion.

The model provides point predictions along with 95 percent confidence intervals for the population mean response and 95 percent prediction intervals for an individual vehicle.  For Scenario A involving a manual transmission with a weight of 2,620 lbs, the point prediction is 24.44 mpg, the 95 percent confidence interval spans from 22.42 to 26.47 mpg, and the 95 percent prediction interval spans from 18.91 to 29.98 mpg.  For Scenario B involving a manual transmission with a weight of 2,800 lbs, the point prediction is 20.85 mpg, the 95 percent confidence interval spans from 19.14 to 22.56 mpg, and the 95 percent prediction interval spans from 15.31 to 26.39 mpg.  

The prediction interval is notably wider than the confidence interval. This occurs because a confidence interval only accounts for the sampling error associated with locating the population mean, whereas a prediction interval must account for both that baseline estimation error and the irreducible individual random variance inherent to predicting a single unique machine.
